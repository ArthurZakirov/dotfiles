# Requirements and scope validation procedure

Evaluate intended changes against the accepted outcome and normal engineering obligations **before** labeling them necessary, supporting, or extra. Neither the user's suggestion nor the agent's initiative establishes that a measure belongs in scope.

1. **Establish the baseline.** Locate the current ticket and its accepted acceptance criteria as the controlling scope for the work. Inspect linked specifications and recorded decisions to interpret those criteria, plus existing behavior, technical contracts, nearby implementations, tests, CI, and applicable engineering standards to understand how to fulfill them. These sources may establish implicit implementation obligations or reveal new needs, but do not silently expand the ticket's accepted behavior. If no accepted ticket scope is recorded, flag the missing baseline for resolution in step 5 rather than inventing it.
2. **Identify intended changes.** Compare the ticket with proposed and accumulated requirements, including a working `requirements.md`, meeting notes, Slack discussions, PR feedback, and ideas from the user or agent. Include behavior, implementation details, tests, validation, error handling, fallbacks, integration work, and cleanup. Preserve each proposal's source and the evidence for it; accumulating or documenting a requirement does not approve it as part of this ticket. Evaluate a coherent change set rather than interrupting every trivial edit.
3. **Determine what belongs in the work.** Ground each material measure in the baseline and distinguish:

   | Classification | Decision basis | Next step |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **Explicitly required** | The current acceptance criteria or a formally accepted update to them requires the behavior. | Check scope fit (step 4), then implement and verify (step 6). |
   | **Implicitly expected engineering work** | A proportionate part of delivering a correct, maintainable change under established repository/team conventions or ordinary applicable engineering standards (for example, focused unit tests for changed logic). The absence of an explicit acceptance criterion is not proof that it is extra. | Confirm the convention and proportionality (step 4); then implement (step 6). |
   | **Technically necessary** | A dependency, invariant, or system constraint demonstrably requires the measure to deliver the accepted outcome; use the smallest sufficient approach. A plausible interpretation alone is not proof. | Check the smallest sufficient approach and scope fit (step 4); then implement (step 6). |
   | **Inferred / disputed requirement** | A behavior, business rule, prerequisite, or quality measure is argued to be implicit in the accepted outcome but is not explicit in the criteria or established by shared engineering standards or demonstrated technical constraints. Reasonable interpretations differ on whether it is necessary. Do not presume either that it belongs in scope or that it is optional; record the competing interpretations and evidence for a scope decision. | Compare interpretations (step 4), then clarify with the accountable parties (step 5); do not implement the disputed part yet. |
   | **Proposed scope expansion** | The measure is established as separate behavior or disproportionate infrastructure, extensive testing, hardening, or refactoring beyond the accepted outcome and normal standards. A stakeholder's request or generic appeal to "best practices" does not authorize that expansion. When necessity is disputed, classify it as **Inferred / disputed requirement** first. | Pause the expansion; route ticket refinement, a separate ticket, or deferral through step 5. |
   | **Unresolved facts or contracts** | The facts or domain rules needed to evaluate or implement the measure are unavailable or unverified, even if its importance is clear. Obtain evidence instead of inventing a mapping, heuristic, or contract. | Obtain evidence or expert input (step 5), then revisit this classification; do not guess. |

4. **Test proportionality and scope fit.** Ask whether the measure is needed to complete and verify the accepted behavior consistently with comparable code, or whether it introduces independently valuable behavior or materially greater review, maintenance, or delivery cost. For example, focused unit tests may be routine completion work; a new end-to-end framework or broad regression suite is not automatically justified. For an inferred or disputed requirement, record the strongest argument for necessity and the alternative interpretation before deciding whether it belongs in the ticket. A stakeholder request or plausible technical approach cannot settle that disagreement on its own; do not prematurely label it either required or scope creep. There is no universal quantitative cutoff: use local evidence and the trade-off.
5. **Clarify and record scope decisions.** If a material change might exceed the accepted outcome but its necessity is uncertain, warn early and explain the uncertainty without calling it confirmed scope creep. If a claimed implicit requirement is disputed, relevant facts are unverified, proposed work expands the accepted criteria, or the baseline is missing, pause only the affected work. Compare the accumulated requirements with the current ticket and surface competing interpretations, evidence gaps, expected outcome, and impact. Seek domain or technical expertise for missing facts; consult the responsible manager, team, or accountable scope owner for ticket scope and prioritization. Neither the agent nor an individual requester should silently expand or reorganize team work:

   | Team decision | Required follow-through |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Refine the current ticket** | Agree on and record revised acceptance criteria, then revisit the estimate and priority before implementing the expanded behavior. |
   | **Create a separate ticket** | Record the new requirement in its own work item with acceptance criteria, refinement, and estimation; keep the current ticket within its existing scope. |
   | **Defer or decline** | Record the decision as appropriate and leave the current ticket unchanged. |

   A stakeholder's authority to identify a requirement is not, by itself, authorization to bypass this ticket-level decision. Resolve factual uncertainty with evidence and contested scope with the appropriate decision-maker; do not assume either that a plausible prerequisite is approved or that it is unnecessary. Do not unilaterally rewrite acceptance criteria, create a new ticket, or implement an unapproved expansion; follow the agreed team process. For a blocking uncertainty, ask the smallest necessary question and continue independent, already-authorized work. Routine implementation choices and proportionate tests do not require manufactured approvals.
6. **Implement and verify agreed work.** Deliver the current acceptance criteria and proportionate engineering obligations. Implement expanded requirements only after the agreed ticket update or separate work item authorizes them. Verify the outcome against the recorded scope and relevant engineering standards without unapproved additions; evaluate new discoveries against the same baseline rather than treating an initial implementation plan as authority.

This procedure decides **what work belongs in scope**. The separately triggered [effort checkpoint procedure](effort-scope-checkpoints.md) assesses **whether the resulting human effort is justified**; do not duplicate its capacity calculations here.
